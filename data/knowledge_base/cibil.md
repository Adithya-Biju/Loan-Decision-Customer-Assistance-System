# CIBIL Score Interpretation Guidelines

> Document type: Synthetic reference policy
> Policy domain: Credit Assessment
> Version: 1.0
> Note: This document is fictional and created for the HDFC Loan Intelligence demonstration system.

## Overview

CIBIL score is a numerical indicator used to understand an applicant's credit history and repayment behavior.

The score should be considered together with income, existing obligations, employment status, previous loans, and default history.

## Score Bands

For this reference system, CIBIL scores may be interpreted as follows:

### Below 650

A score below 650 indicates elevated credit risk.

Applications in this range should receive additional scrutiny, particularly when combined with:

- High DTI.
- Existing defaults.
- Unstable employment.
- High loan-to-income ratio.
- Significant existing EMI obligations.

A score below 650 does not by itself mean automatic rejection.

### 650 to 749

A score between 650 and 749 represents a moderate credit profile.

The applicant should still be assessed using income, repayment capacity, employment stability, and previous loan behavior.

### 750 and Above

A score of 750 or higher represents a strong credit profile under this reference framework.

However, a strong CIBIL score does not guarantee approval.

## Credit History

Credit history should be assessed alongside the score.

Relevant factors include:

- Number of previous loans.
- Repayment performance.
- Default history.
- Age of credit history.
- Existing obligations.

## Defaults

A recorded default is a significant negative factor.

When defaults are present, the reviewer should determine:

- Number of defaults.
- Recency where available.
- Whether obligations were subsequently resolved.
- Current repayment behavior.
- Overall financial capacity.

## Missing Credit Information

If CIBIL or credit history is unavailable, the system should not invent a score.

The case should be marked as having incomplete credit information and may require manual verification.

## Decision Guidance

CIBIL should be treated as one component of a broader credit assessment rather than as a standalone approval or rejection rule.
